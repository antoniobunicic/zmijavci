All Saints Church

A stone parish church on Tonkića glavica, built in 1895 on the site of an older church. Inside are a Tyrolean wooden altar, an old crucifix and eleven stained-glass windows by Branimir Dorotić.

Built
1895
Location
Tonkića glavica
Feast day
1 November
Protection
Cultural property

On Tonkića glavica, a hill that has held a special place in the life of Zmijavci for centuries, rises the parish church of All Saints, one of the most important religious and historic monuments of the area. Its story speaks of a long religious tradition, of community, and of the devotion of the generations who built and kept the spiritual identity of the village.

The present church was built in 1895, when Friar Ante Rako was parish priest, on the site of an older church that had been enlarged in 1821 through the efforts of Friar Andrija Šabić and 28 families of Zmijavci. Historical sources mention a small church or chapel dedicated to St Anthony of Padua here as early as the mid-18th century, so the sacred history of the site reaches well beyond today's building.

The church is built of carefully dressed stone blocks as a single-nave building about 26 metres long and 11 metres wide. Its façade features a stone portal carved with the year of construction, a round window and a bell gable with three bells. The simple lines and harmony of the stonework reflect the tradition of church building in the Dalmatian hinterland.

The interior is distinguished by a large wooden altar of Tyrolean workmanship, acquired in 1903, a fine example of the church art of its time. The church also keeps an old wooden crucifix, and its interior is further enriched by eleven stained-glass windows by the painter Branimir Dorotić, installed in 1999.

The church has been restored and refurbished several times. A particularly important renovation took place from 1976 to 1978, when the sanctuary was adapted to modern liturgical needs. In 2002 a new bell tower was built beside the church, further shaping its present appearance.

A turning point in the religious life of Zmijavci came in 1982, when the independent parish of All Saints was founded. Until then, the people of Zmijavci had belonged at different times to the parishes of Podbablje and Runovići. With a parish of its own, the church took an even more central place in the daily life of the community.

The most festive day in Zmijavci is 1 November, the feast of All Saints, when the parish celebrates its patrons. The day brings together locals, their families and the many people from Zmijavci who live elsewhere, a lasting bond between the village, its people and its faith.

Today All Saints Church is a protected cultural property of the Republic of Croatia. Beyond its religious role, it is a valuable monument of built heritage and an essential stop for anyone getting to know the history of Zmijavci. Its stone walls, artistic details and story tell of a community that has kept its faith, customs and sense of belonging through the centuries.
