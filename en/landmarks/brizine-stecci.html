Stećci at Brižine

A medieval cemetery of at least 31 stećci, the carved medieval tombstones, set on a prehistoric burial mound. One bears a carved cross, another an unusual image of two figures holding hands.

Site
Brižine–Balinjača, near the hamlet of Milasi
Stećci
14th – 15th century
Number of stećci
at least 31
Protection
Cultural property Z-6491

On the hill of Brižine, north-west of the hamlet of Milasi in Zmijavci, lies the archaeological site of Brižine–Balinjača, which preserves traces of life and burial customs from several periods of history. The site is best known for its stećci: medieval stone tombstones typical of present-day Croatia and its neighbouring countries.

The history of this place reaches far beyond the Middle Ages. The oldest known layer is a prehistoric burial mound, or tumulus, about 20 metres across. In the 14th and 15th centuries a medieval cemetery with stećci grew up on and around it, giving the place an important role in the life of the local community once again.

At least 31 stećci have been recorded here, mostly stone slabs and chests, along with gabled tombstones shaped like the roof of a house. Most are plain and roughly worked, but two stand out: one is carved with a cross, while the other shows two figures holding hands. This unusual motif draws particular attention, inviting reflection on the relationships, symbols and customs of the medieval people who lived here.

The story of Brižine did not end with the Middle Ages. In the early modern period a new cemetery developed on the same ground, marked by simple stone grave markers known locally as učelci. Over the centuries the hill has remained a place for remembering earlier generations.

Today Brižine–Balinjača is a protected cultural property of the Republic of Croatia and a valuable part of the archaeological heritage of the Imotski region. It is special not only for the number of surviving stećci, but because layers of history from prehistory to the early modern period can be traced in a single place.

The stećci at Brižine are a reminder of Zmijavci's rich past and of the lasting bond between people and the land they live on, leaving testimony in stone that outlives the centuries.
