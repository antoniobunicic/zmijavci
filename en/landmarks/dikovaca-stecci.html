Stećci at Dikovača

A 290-metre hilltop with eight medieval stećci decorated with a crescent moon, a rosette, a lily and a kolo circle dance. Traces of settlement go back to the Bronze and Iron Ages.

approx. 290 m

On the prominent hill of Dikovača, about 290 metres above sea level, lies one of the most interesting archaeological sites in Zmijavci. Important to local people for thousands of years, this place preserves traces of prehistoric communities, of the Roman period and of medieval heritage.

Dikovača is especially valued for its stećci, the monumental medieval stone tombstones typical of the Imotski region and the wider Dinaric area. In the Middle Ages a cemetery with graves of various forms grew up on the flattened hilltop, its slopes and the surrounding ground, the most striking of them marked with stećci.

Eight graves covered with stećci have been recorded here: five gabled tombstones, one chest and two slabs. The gabled stones are especially recognisable, shaped like the roof of a house, while the chests and slabs are simpler stone forms.

Some of the Dikovača stećci were decorated with symbolic images, among them a crescent moon, a rosette, a stylised lily and a kolo, the traditional circle dance. These motifs are a precious trace of the medieval art and spiritual world of the people who once lived here.

Yet Dikovača's historical importance reaches far beyond its medieval necropolis. Archaeological finds, including fragments of Bronze and Iron Age pottery and remains of building material, confirm that the hill was used in prehistory. On the summit is a flattened plateau of irregular oval shape, along whose edges parts of stone structures survive that may be the remains of an old defensive wall.

Today Dikovača is a protected cultural property of the Republic of Croatia and an important part of the archaeological heritage of Zmijavci. Its stećci and other remains recall the generations who shaped the identity of this area over the centuries, leaving behind valuable traces preserved in stone.
